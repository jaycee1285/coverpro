## The Positioning

PMM for developer tools is the true hybrid. You need to understand the product deeply enough that developers trust your messaging, AND you need to drive pipeline. Most PMMs are either "marketers who learned some tech" (developers smell this immediately) or "engineers who tolerate marketing" (can't drive pipeline). John is neither — he builds the products AND drives the pipeline.

The resume must prove:

1. **You understand the product at an architectural level** — Not just features. Trade-offs, edge cases, why it works this way.
2. **You drive measurable pipeline** — Qualified leads, revenue attribution, not vanity metrics.
3. **You can write positioning developers don't hate** — Specific, honest, trade-off-aware. Zero "revolutionary platform" energy.

The differentiator from content mode: Content strategist writes the content. PMM owns the positioning, messaging, competitive analysis, and launch strategy — content is one output, not the job.

The differentiator from PM: PM owns the product roadmap. PMM owns how the product is perceived, positioned, and sold.

## Canonical Bullet Strategy

### Gestallt (2 bullets — select from PM canonical set)

Best pairing for PMM:
- "Architected RBAC with cryptographic JWT isolation for team switching, preventing cross-client data access."
- "Designed tenant-scoped search with 1-hour key expiry—traded brief cache exposure for simpler architecture."

**Why**: Bullet 1 shows you understand the product deeply. Bullet 2 shows you understand trade-offs AND can articulate them clearly — which is literally what PMM competitive messaging requires. "We chose X over Y because Z" is the core of technical product positioning.

### CoverPro (1 bullet)

Best single bullet for PMM:
- "Designed model routing—Sonnet for drafting, Opus for critique—balances quality and cost per task."

**Why**: Shows AI/LLM domain knowledge (relevant for developer tools companies in the AI space) and cost optimization thinking (relevant for PMMs who need to understand pricing and packaging).

**Alternative**: "Architected multi-backend pipeline supporting 3 agent and 3 API backends—cost-optimized switching." (if JD emphasizes platform/vendor strategy)

### ContextMax (1 bullet)

Best single bullet for PMM:
- "Designed knowledge transfer protocol reducing agent onboarding context 80%—interview-driven docs pipeline."

**Why**: Developer onboarding IS product marketing for devtools. Getting a developer from "never heard of you" to "productive with your product" is the PMM challenge. This bullet shows you've solved it systematically.

### labDemand (2 bullets — reuse from FME canonical set)

Best pairing for PMM:
- "Built demand gen program for ISO 17025 labs from zero—mapped test methods to buyer intent across verticals."
- "Lifted client from page 2 to page 1, sourcing $25K contract from organic within first quarter."

**Why**: Bullet 1 shows 0→1 GTM ownership. Bullet 2 shows revenue attribution. Together they say "I built the pipeline from nothing and it produced revenue." That's what PMM hiring managers want to see.

### Focus Digital (3 bullets — hybrid framing)

**Critical framing for PMM**: Not pure SEO metrics (content mode) and not pure pipeline reframing (FME mode). PMM needs BOTH — metrics that show you can drive pipeline AND framing that shows you think about positioning.

**Recommended bullets:**
- "Drove 14 qualified leads/month for RF testing lab—$25K-$75K contracts from organic, zero paid spend."
- "Built content structures (TL;DR blocks, proof patterns, AEO FAQs) lifting engagement 2.4x across verticals."
- "Managed competitive positioning across six industry verticals—adapted messaging per buyer persona and market."

**Selection notes**: Bullet 1 is the pipeline metric. Bullet 2 shows content architecture (PMM owns content strategy). Bullet 3 shows competitive positioning and multi-segment messaging — core PMM skills. This is the only mode where the third FD bullet explicitly mentions competitive positioning.

### Lear Marketing (1 bullet)

**Reframe for**: Positioning technical products for founders who can't articulate their own value prop.

- "Developed market positioning for technical founders—translated product capabilities into buyer-facing messaging."

**Why**: This is the PMM job. Founders build things but can't explain why anyone should care. You translate. The fact that you did this for electric aircraft propulsion and DOE solid-state lighting proves you can do it for any technical product.

## Cover Letter Angles (WAR Format)

### PMM-specific plays:

**Play 1 (strongest)**: Product depth as positioning advantage. "Most PMMs learn your product from demos and docs. I build products like yours. Gestallt is a multi-tenant RBAC platform with HIPAA compliance. CoverPro is a multi-agent AI pipeline with model routing and quality gates. When I write positioning for [company], I understand the architecture — which means I understand the trade-offs, the edge cases, and the honest differentiators. Developers can tell when marketing understands the product. I do."

**Play 2**: Pipeline proof. "At Focus Digital, I drove 14 qualified leads/month for an RF testing lab — $25K-$75K contracts from organic content alone. No paid spend. The content structures I built (TL;DR blocks, proof patterns, AEO FAQs) lifted engagement 2.4x. At labDemand, I built the entire demand gen program from zero for ISO 17025 labs. I don't theorize about pipeline — I build it."

**Play 3**: Developer-honest positioning. "Developer tools marketing has a credibility problem. Developers ignore messaging that doesn't understand their world. My approach: lead with honest trade-offs, not feature lists. When I document that Gestallt's search keys have a 1-hour TTL because we traded perfect revocation for simpler architecture, that's the kind of transparency developers trust. I'd bring that same honesty to [company]'s positioning."

**Close**: "I'd like to discuss how my product development background and pipeline track record could strengthen [company]'s developer marketing. Available [timeframe]."

### PMM cover letter rules:
- Name the company and their primary product
- Reference their developer audience and a specific positioning challenge
- Include BOTH a project (product depth) and a metric (pipeline proof)
- Tone: confident, specific, market-aware. Not "I'm passionate about developer marketing."
- Show you understand developers as buyers (not just users)
- Developer tools PMM should never sound like enterprise SaaS PMM

- "SEO strategist" or "SEO execution" as primary framing
- "Content writer" or "copywriter" as self-descriptor (use "product marketer" or "positioning")
- Toyota/eBay-specific claims
- "Revolutionary" or "cutting-edge" or any developer-repelling marketing speak

### Character rules:
- Same as all modes: 80-110 characters per bullet, AP style numbers, period at end

## Summary Line Guidance

**PMM summary should emphasize**: Product marketer who understands developer products because he builds them. Pipeline-driven, positioning-focused.

**Template**: "Product marketer who builds developer tools and drives pipeline—14 qualified leads/month from organic positioning."

**Anti-pattern**: Don't say "bridge between product and marketing" (cliché). Don't say "technical marketer" (undersells). Lead with what you DO: build products, drive pipeline.
